Signals that count what is actually on the road.

SmartChowk is an adaptive Edge-AI traffic signal controller built for Indian mixed traffic. Queue sensors and YOLOv8 vision set green time to 15, 25 or 35 seconds, an ESP32 running FreeRTOS drives the signal, and a 433 MHz RF override clears the way for ambulances.

The problem at the junction

New Delhi records 60.2% congestion and 104 hours lost in rush-hour traffic (TomTom Traffic Index 2025).

Timers ignore demand

Fixed-cycle signals run the same plan whether the road is empty or jammed, so green time is wasted on empty approaches while queues grow on busy ones.

Traffic keeps changing

Volume shifts by hour, weekday and city event. A single timing plan cannot follow it.

Mixed traffic breaks standard models

Two-wheelers, autos, cars, buses and trucks share the same lanes. Global models and basic sensors assume cleaner, car-only flow.

People and air pay for it

Long gridlocks mean idling engines, wasted fuel and worse urban air quality.

Current automation has gaps

Cloud systems add latency, raise privacy concerns and fail when the internet drops. Pixel-counting gets fooled by shadows and weather.

Our approach

Run detection on the device at the intersection, count vehicles by type, and let queue length and weighted density decide the signal.

How it works

Every decision happens at the intersection, with no round trip to a server.

  1. Overhead camera and sensors

    A live lane feed goes to the embedded board, while IR sensors at 25, 60 and 100 m mark how far the queue reaches.

  2. YOLOv8 detection

    Detects and classifies two-wheelers, autos, cars, buses and trucks, robust to shadows and weather because it recognises objects, not pixel changes.

  3. Queue and density estimate

    Vehicles inside each approach's detection zone are counted and weighted by size into one number per approach.

  4. Adaptive controller

    Triggered sensors map to LOW, MED or HIGH density, which sets a 15, 25 or 35 second green. A lane that empties early hands over sooner.

  5. ESP32 signal controller

    The density class goes to an ESP32 running FreeRTOS, which switches the relay driving the signal heads and enforces amber and all-red clearance. If vision is obstructed, the ESP32 falls back to a fixed timer on its own.

Why it stands out

Adaptive

Green time steps through 15, 25 and 35 seconds as the measured queue grows, so timing follows real demand.

Low-cost

The working prototype costs ₹1,680 and uses off-the-shelf parts. Sensor trigger, density class, signal change: simple to demo.

Deployable

Surface-mounted sensing needs no road excavation, and the RF override makes the junction emergency-ready.

From queue to green time

The prototype reads the queue with three IR sensors. The vision layer refines it: a bus takes far more road than a scooter, so YOLOv8 counts each class with a passenger car unit (PCU) weight.

Phase decision rule

The same rule runs in the simulator above.

sensors S1 S2 S3 at 25 / 60 / 100 m

1 triggered -> LOW  -> 15 s green
2 triggered -> MED  -> 25 s green
3 triggered -> HIGH -> 35 s green

early switch when the green lane empties
always: amber 2 s, then all-red 1.5 s
433 MHz RF interrupt -> preemption

Roadmap and impact

Next four steps

  1. Calibrate

    Camera height, lane ROIs and queue thresholds.

  2. Validate

    Test detection accuracy on Indian mixed traffic.

  3. Close the loop

    YOLOv8 to Arduino to the signal controller model.

  4. Pilot

    Run controlled junction tests and log delay and queue metrics.

Evidence to collect: mean delay, 95th-percentile wait, queue length, false detections.

What it changes

Shorter waits and shorter queues, so less idling and less fuel burned at the junction.

Lower emissions where people actually breathe: at the stop line.

No continuous video upload, so privacy is built in, and the junction keeps working offline.

Low cost per intersection makes city-wide rollout realistic.

Ambulances and fire engines get a green corridor through a local 433 MHz override.
